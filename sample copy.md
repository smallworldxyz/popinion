# The Cambodian People's Party

The Cambodian People's Party (CPP), under Hun Sen, has been the defacto ruling party since the 1997, after Hun Sen contested an election a few years earlier in 1993 that saw him share power with then co-Prime Minister, Ranariddh Sihanouk, of the FUNCINPEC party.

On paper, Prime Minister Hun Sen's government had instated a multi-party system under a constitutional monarchy. The Monarch, King Norodom Sihamoni, was crowned king in 2004, after Norodom Sihanouk's abdication. His position and role is purely symbolic. Multi-parties were indeed represented in the Senate and National Assembly, but made up an insignificant minority compared to the CPP.

The CPP's coup d'etat of 1997 required extremely strong support from military and police assets, which not only allowed the CPP to take power but also to retain undisputed control. In the days following the coup, non-CPP members, primarily FUNCINPEC members, lost their positions within military and government, or had senior positions filled with members of the CPP party. This gave the CPP all key positions of power and decision making.

Business tycoons, many whom benefited from the post-war / post-UNTAC reconstruction efforts, were also allies of the CPP. Main efforts for the reconstruction of every industry were split between few key business figures.

Cambodia's reconstruction, growth and stability is built on the intermarriage between business, military, police and politics. Much of the country's wealth is created and circulated among a small circle of elites linking back to a deep web tying key CPP members and allies.

Much of those in power within the military were allies of Hun Sen. Through the years, positions within government and military were shared among family dynasties, almost as a way to ensure dynastic continuation, even through marriage. Economic power was also consolidated within just a handful of families, all with ties to the Hun family. In 2023, power was passed over from Hun Sen to his son Hun Manet via a party nomination and unanimous support from senior party members, and finally an election process.

Despite no longer being prime minister, Hun Sen, as acting president of the CPP and advisor to the king, making him extremely active in party politics and thus country political directions.
